Undergraduate thesis

April 2025 – June 2026

Effects of Weber Number and Film Thickness on Droplet Dynamics on Thin Liquid Films

Computational Fluid Dynamics · Multiphase Flow · Droplet Dynamics · Numerical Modelling

A numerical investigation of droplet impact on thin liquid films, focusing on crown evolution, transient pressure loading, and velocity-field development. The study examines how impact inertia and liquid-film thickness influence the redistribution of momentum during impact.

Research objectives

  • Investigate how Weber number and liquid-film thickness influence crown evolution and impact-momentum redistribution.
  • Characterize transient pressure loading, velocity fields, and radial crown propagation through validated CFD simulations.
  • Use CFD-generated crown-radius trajectories to explore a subsequent data-driven predictive model.

Methodology and Numerical Framework

  • Developed a transient 2D axisymmetric CFD model in ANSYS Fluent using the Finite Volume Method (FVM).
  • Used Volume of Fluid (VOF) interface tracking with surface tension to capture the evolving liquid–air interface.
  • Applied dynamic Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) to resolve interfacial deformation and important flow features.
  • Examined crown morphology, pressure distribution, velocity fields, and dimensionless crown propagation across the investigated impact conditions.

CFD Results and Discussion

Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR)

Local mesh refinement concentrates resolution near the evolving droplet interface. This captures interfacial deformation and important flow features while retaining a coarser mesh away from the interface.

Crown Contour

Film thickness changes vertical crown development and momentum redistribution: shallower films show more pronounced normalized crown growth. Impact inertia, expressed through Weber number, governs the characteristic radial spreading scales. These contour sequences compare film thickness at a fixed Weber number.

Pressure Field

The strongest pressure gradients develop near the impact region as downward momentum is redirected. Thicker films distribute the pressure response more broadly, reducing local peak pressure and extending relaxation. These fields are numerical predictions from the crown-validated model.

Velocity Vector

Impact redirects liquid motion outward from the contact region, producing radial flow and initiating crown development. Increasing impact inertia strengthens local velocities; the overall flow pattern remains broadly similar across the investigated cases.

Undergraduate thesis · Subsequent data-driven extension

Data-Driven Modelling: Augmented Neural ODE

Following the CFD investigation, I used the CFD-generated crown-radius trajectories to develop an Augmented Neural Ordinary Differential Equation surrogate model for the time-dependent crown rim radius, Rc(t), during droplet impact on thin liquid films. The objective was to investigate whether a data-driven surrogate could reproduce CFD-derived crown dynamics and offer a faster predictive alternative to repeated numerical simulations.

Conventional Neural ODEs can struggle with complex, non-monotonic trajectories in a restricted state space. Augmenting crown rim radius with two latent state variables provides additional representational flexibility for these dynamics.
